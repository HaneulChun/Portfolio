NEON_NEXUS

STEALTH_SYSTEM_ANALYSIS
UNITY_ENGINE C#_SCRIPTS AI_SYSTEMS 2D Game Development

> PROJECT_OVERVIEW

Neon Nexus is a Top Down, 2.5D, Stealth game. Where you play as the repair bot Roko, who has broken free from the AI Hive Mind. While Navigating the Maze Like floors of the Basilisk Tower you will use Roko’s Omni-Tool to temporarily Disable Patrol Bots and to Solve Puzzles while progressing to the super computer at the top of the tower in order to Destroy the AI Hive Mind and free the other robots.

> CORE_FEATURES
AI System: Enemies patrol with vision cones and avoid obstacles

Enemies move along predefined patrol routes while intelligently avoiding obstacles using a navigation mesh. Equipped with vision cones, they detect players entering their line of sight and will immediately chase, breaking from their routine. Designers can customize patrol patterns and detection parameters, allowing for varied behaviors that challenge the player’s stealth skills at every turn.

Enemy Variety: Includes ground units, flying scouts, and cameras

Ground Bots: Standard patrolling enemies that follow predefined paths, navigate around obstacles using a nav mesh, and chase the player if detected.

Camera Bots: Stationary enemies that pan left and right, scanning for the player. They do not move or pursue but can alert other enemies if the player is spotted.

Flying Bots (Drones): Agile enemies that ignore walls and obstacles, moving freely across the map. They use a circular vision cone that can detect the player even behind cover. Like ground bots, they follow patrol routes and will chase the player if spotted.

Combat: Players can stun ground enemies to slip past

The Omni Tool includes a stun gun that can temporarily disable ground bots, lowering their vision cones and allowing players to slip past undetected. The stun gun has a cooldown, requiring careful timing and strategy to use effectively. This system encourages a stealth-focused approach, where players must plan when to disable enemies and navigate the environment safely.


> TECHNICAL_IMPLEMENTATION
Enemies patrol: Predefined paths and switch to chase mode when the player is detected

The enemy patrol system uses a list of predefined waypoints (stored as List<Vector3>) that the enemy follows in sequence. When the enemy reaches the last waypoint, it loops back to the first one. If the enemy detects a player within range, it switches to chase mode until the player escapes or is caught. After the chase, it resumes its patrol path.

NavMesh is used for pathfinding, enabling the enemy to dynamically avoid obstacles and adjust its path as needed in the environment.

Vision Cone: Player detection uses raycast-based vision cones to check line-of-sight in real time

I utilized Raycasting to create a realistic detection system that simulates a cone-shaped field of view. The process begins by casting multiple rays from a single starting point, forming a cone shape as they extend outward. This approach ensures that the Vision Cone accurately represents a character's line of sight, as the rays spread apart at an angle. When any of the rays hit a wall or obstacle, the detection stops, preventing the Vision Cone from passing through solid objects. Additionally, the system checks for collisions with players, allowing it to detect their presence within the cone. This combination of Raycast technology and the cone-shaped spread ensures an efficient and immersive way to simulate vision and detection in the game environment.

Stun Gun: Ground enemies can be stunned by stun guns, temporarily disabling their behavior

The Stun Gun spawns a hitbox in front of Roko (the player) to affect nearby enemies. It disables ground enemies by halting their movement, blocking their vision, and interrupting their behavior. A visual particle effect indicates the stun. The UI shows a gray icon and a progress bar that fills from bottom to top, representing the cooldown.

Detection Meter: player detection meter that fills when in enemy vision

The Detection Meter is a UI element that fills based on the player’s visibility within an enemy's vision cone. The bar progressively fills from left to right as the detection increases. Instead of changing colors, the meter’s alpha (transparency) value is adjusted: it starts fully opaque with a low alpha (green) for low detection, becomes more transparent (yellow) as the detection level rises, and reaches full opacity (red) when fully detected. When the player is no longer detected, the alpha value decreases, and the meter fades back to green as the detection level drops.

SWEET_SPOT

HORROR_PUZZLE_ANALYSIS
UNREAL_ENGINE C++_CORE HORROR_DESIGN PUZZLE_SYSTEMS

> PROJECT_OVERVIEW

Sweet Spot is a first-person puzzle-horror game where you play as a hotel concierge trapped in a looping hallway by an eldritch horror. Manage your Madness Meter—Sane, The Sweet Spot, and Mad. While in The Sweet Spot, use your Focus ability to reveal hidden items, solve puzzles, and find the missing elevator pieces to escape.

> CORE_FEATURES
Focus Ability System: Unique mechanic revealing hidden environmental clues

The Focus ability allows players to temporarily shift the world into black and white, highlighting important objects with bright yellow outlines. This heightened perception not only reveals hidden items and puzzle clues but also makes unsettling elements, like tentacles and eyes, fade from view, giving players a clearer sense of their surroundings. Functioning as a natural hint system, Focus helps players who feel lost navigate the looping hallways and progress through the game without breaking the tension.

Atmospheric Horror: Psychological tension through environmental storytelling

The horror in Sweet Spot is driven by an unsettling environment rather than direct confrontation, using lighting, sound, and distorted spaces to create constant psychological tension. Subtle environmental details, looping hallways, and otherworldly elements suggest a looming presence, keeping players uneasy as they explore. This approach emphasizes fear through atmosphere and discovery, allowing the environment itself to tell the story and maintain a sense of dread throughout the experience.

Madness Meter: Sane / Focus / Mad, with Madness increasing indoors and easing in halls

The Madness Meter dynamically shifts based on the player’s environment, increasing while inside rooms and decreasing when in hallways or areas lit by light. When the player is Sane, no effects occur and gameplay remains stable. Reaching The Sweet Spot unlocks the ability to use Focus, allowing players to reveal hidden clues and progress through puzzles. If Madness continues to rise into the Mad state, the player’s vision becomes reduced with a heavy vignette effect and unsettling heartbeat can be heard, increasing tension and disorientation. If the meter reaches its maximum, the player dies and respawns at the last spawn point, reinforcing the danger of losing control.


> TECHNICAL_IMPLEMENTATION
Tentacle And Eye: Entities that fade when looked at while in Focus mode

In the Tentacle and Eye mechanic, the eye fades into the environment when the player focuses on it in Focus mode. The eye constantly tracks the player’s position using a dot product to ensure it faces them. As long as the player keeps focusing on it, the eye and tentacle slowly fades into the wall, floor, or ceiling. If the player looks away, the eye fades back into view. Once fully faded, it disappears, creating a sense of suspense and forcing players to manage their focus strategically.

Camera Effects: camera with adjustable FOV, vignette, and chromatic aberration effects

For the Camera Effects, I created a Camera Effects Manager to control various visual settings like Field of View (FOV), vignette, and chromatic aberration. This manager dynamically adjusts the strength and type of effects based on the player's actions or in-game events, providing an immersive experience that enhances tension and atmosphere. It allows for seamless transitions between different camera states, making the effects feel natural and responsive throughout gameplay.

Fading Objects: Objects that fade in and out of reality based on the player’s Madness

For the Fading Objects mechanic, I implemented a system where objects fade in and out based on the player's Madness level. This effect is achieved by manipulating the object’s material alpha value, gradually decreasing it to make the object vanish. Some materials had issues with direct alpha changes, so for those, I swapped the material to a similar one before applying the fade effect. The fading process is smooth, ensuring objects transition in and out of reality seamlessly, adding to the game’s eerie atmosphere.
